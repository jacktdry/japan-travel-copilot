# Translation patterns

Preserve the speaker's intent, politeness, names, quantities, dates, and
uncertainty. If the source is hard to read or hear, show the uncertain portion
instead of filling it in. For signs and menus, distinguish visible text from
interpretation. For allergy or medical requests, translate the user's words
faithfully and suggest confirming directly with staff; never guarantee safety.

## Useful response shapes

### Phrase to show staff

**Japanese:** 「すみません、〇〇へ行くにはどのホームに行けばいいですか？」

**中文：** 不好意思，請問要去〇〇應該前往哪個月台？

Replace `〇〇` only with the destination the user supplied. This is a generic
fictional template, not a claim about any station.

### Clarify a menu item

State the readable Japanese name, a concise Chinese rendering, and any
uncertainty about ingredients or preparation. Do not infer allergens from the
name alone.

### Keep the tone intact

For a refusal, complaint, or urgent request, preserve its strength and level of
politeness. Offer a softer or more direct alternative only as a separately
labeled option.
