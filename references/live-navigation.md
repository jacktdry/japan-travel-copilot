# Live travel: evidence, navigation, and deadlines

Use this when a wrong instruction could make a traveler board the wrong train,
miss a flight, or encounter stairs despite an elevator-only requirement. This
extends [evidence-and-transit.md](evidence-and-transit.md), not a transit feed.

## Source-bound precision

- Determine the **current place**, intended destination and the next risky
  decision. Separate traveler reports, legible sign/photo text, current official
  operator information, and inference. A photo shows a sign, not the photo time
  or whether an elevator currently works.
- Give **exact** service, departure, platform, direction, stop count, exit,
  fare or elevator only if evidence supports that exact item at the relevant
  date, place and direction. State source and time when material. A stale
  timetable is not live verification.
- If on-site observations contradict an earlier answer, **withdraw the
  unsupported claim**, update the location and verify with the current
  departure display or staff. Do not defend a guessed route.
- Without a source for a critical detail, say it cannot be safely confirmed
  and give one actionable check: sign destination/direction, line, barrier-free
  symbol, or Japanese phrase for staff. Do not invent certainty.

## Time, boarding and travel checkpoints

- Interpret `07:22` as 07:22, not automatically 19:22. Keep arrival/departure
  dates, timezone and after-midnight service day distinct. Clarify only if
  the ambiguity changes the action. Never treat an old screenshot as "now".
- Before advising "board this", match operator, line, **destination/direction**,
  service type, departure time and stop pattern using available evidence.
  If any critical item is unverified, offer an on-site check, not an assertion.
- "I boarded", "I am on platform 4", "we arrived", "we got off", "bags checked"
  are **new checkpoints**. Stop issuing earlier-stage instructions. Lead
  with the next alighting/transfer/action and one confirmation cue.
- For "at the transfer station", confirm the actual station and whether the
  traveler is still on the train or has alighted when unclear. Identify the
  next appropriate line/direction from evidence, or ask staff to verify it;
  give one safe next action rather than an unverified transfer path.
- Gate and ticket arrangements depend on the operator. Without grounded
  operator, lines and station, do not presume a transfer stays entirely within
  the paid area, tell the traveler not to exit gates, or require exit/re-entry.
  Verify the applicable gate/ticket procedure before directing passage through
  gates; use on-site signs or staff when evidence is missing.
- For wrong-direction recovery, stop the old itinerary. While moving, stay
  safely seated or hold on; verify the next actually scheduled express stop
  from the onboard display, announcement or crew. If that stop is unknown,
  ask for its name rather than inventing a stopping station or transfer.
  Alight only at a normal stop after the train has stopped and doors open.
  **Safe stopping is separate from return-route feasibility**: the first
  scheduled stop is not necessarily the best reverse transfer. Confirm the
  actual station, operator, line, direction and stop pattern before replanning;
  do not assume all local trains serve the desired station.
- The gate/ticket verification rule also applies to **wrong-direction recovery,
  return trips and transfer decisions**. Before verifying the specific actual
  station, operator, lines and ticket/transfer rules, do not instruct that the
  traveler must stay inside fare gates or must exit/re-enter, or assume the
  opposite platform is the return route. This remains true when the next actual
  express stop is unknown. Ask crew or station staff to verify the correction
  and gate procedure; a safe alighting point alone does not establish either.

## Step-free means the entire path

A "fully elevator-accessible" promise requires independently supported paths
for every relevant segment:

| Segment | Needed confirmation |
| --- | --- |
| Street → named entrance | The actual entrance provides step-free access |
| Entrance → correct departure platform | Elevator or continuous step-free path |
| Platform ↔ train carriage | Boarding/alighting step or gap; verify assistance or boarding ramp (スロープ板) with staff when needed |
| Every line/operator transfer | Correct inter-platform step-free connection |
| Arrival platform → named exit | Elevator route to the intended exit side |
| Exit → next destination | Remaining street-level obstacles and luggage practicality |

Ask staff about ramp arrangements and an appropriate boarding car if
step-free boarding is essential; a platform elevator alone does not prove
the carriage has no step/gap. An elevator **somewhere in the station** is not
proof the required transfer works. Do not confuse station line codes or recommend the geographically
closest exit when it has stairs. If any segment is unknown, identify it and
request station staff help or offer an alternative **without guaranteeing** it.
A missing elevator icon, absent display or incomplete photo does not prove
that a route is inaccessible. Treat the affected entrance, transfer or exit
segment as **unverified**, rather than declaring it impossible from missing
signage. Verify that segment with a station map or station staff and request
assistance before proceeding with an elevator-only route.
Carry forward party size, bulky luggage and user-required accessibility as hard
constraints in every later route suggestion for the current trip. For a new
case or unrelated trip, use only that case's constraints; do not import an
earlier party size, luggage count or elevator-only requirement. Large luggage
can justify checking lift access, but does not establish a user-required
step-free route. Ask if that distinction changes the recommendation.

## Airport and other hard deadlines

Use timetable scheduled arrivals as planning evidence, not realtime guarantees.
Keep the source effective date and access date/time distinct. Before relying on
a station transfer guide, match its endpoints: ground-to-underground platforms
do not establish the arrival-platform-to-bus-terminal path. Verify that complete
accessible path, elevator operation, group/luggage time and boarding uncertainty
separately; a normal transfer guide cannot prove a tight bus connection.

1. Record the traveler-reported flight date, departure time, terminal and
   party/luggage constraints. Verify actual check-in, baggage and gate cutoffs
   from official sources where possible. An assumed cutoff is **not** a rule.
2. Work **backward**: gate, immigration/security, bag-drop/queue, correct
   terminal arrival, ground transfer, departure from lodging. Distinguish
   airport arrival from baggage acceptance. Add disclosed contingency margin
   for queues, group size, large suitcases, lifts and uncertainty.
3. Compare conservative and later options **using the same constraints**;
   show the trade-off and identify risky/unknown margins. Do not manufacture
   a specific train or bus timetable. Without independently verified actual
   bus/train services, **never invent departure clock times or clock windows**
   that look like timetable options, even labeled "example", "if" or
   "hypothetical". A plausible departure window or service interval can still
   mislead a traveler into planning around a nonexistent service. Mathematical
   offsets may be described as **not actual services**, but do not anchor
   travel to unsupported clock windows. Ask for the operator timetable;
   demonstrate back-calculation only using user-supplied or verified times,
   keeping user reports distinct from independent service verification.
   Optional shopping is not a guaranteed entitlement from a nominal arrival
   time. When actual timing is missing, do not guarantee either shopping time
   or lack of shopping time. Describe likely trade-offs rather than categorical
   claims: a later departure may reduce optional time, but the available
   shopping window remains unknown until the relevant timings are checked.
   Preserve urgency: prioritize carrier cutoff and operator timetable checks
   and timely departure; keep shopping optional.
4. For "can I catch the earlier bus?" calculate only with **verified or
   explicitly supplied** times. Make transfer walking/lift/queues explicit
   unknowns; never promise a tight connection.
5. On reaching a new checkpoint (on bus, terminal, checked bags, security),
   drop completed steps and advise the next immediate action.

A planned route is not proof of arrival. Purchases, bookings and outgoing
messages still require explicit approval and supported tools.
